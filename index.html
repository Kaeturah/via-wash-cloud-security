01 / Business context

A business scenario with real security challenges.

Via Wash is modelled as a growing laundry and dry-cleaning organisation. The architecture begins with London and is designed to support expansion across the UK and potentially internationally.

The challenge

Growing complexity

Expansion introduces more employees, locations, contractors, applications and access decisions. Poorly managed permissions can increase security risk.

The requirement

Controlled access

Employees should receive access according to their responsibilities. A job title should never provide unrestricted access by default.

The objective

Secure scalability

Develop an identity architecture that can support new locations and business functions without continuously redesigning the security model.

02 / Identity architecture

Start with identities and responsibilities.

The initial design separates operational roles into security groups. These groups establish the identity structure but do not automatically grant access to applications or resources.

03 / Security principles

Security decisions driven by business needs.

The architecture is being developed around the risks Via Wash would face as its workforce, locations and technology environment grow.

Least privilege

Give each identity only the permissions required for its responsibilities. Avoid unnecessary administrative privileges.

Identity lifecycle

Consider how identities are created, changed, reviewed and disabled when access is no longer required.

Separation of duties

Separate operational responsibilities from sensitive functions such as payroll, finance and security administration.

Defence in depth

Build toward layered controls including strong authentication, access policies, privileged access management and monitoring.

04 / Implementation roadmap

From business requirement to tested control.

Capabilities are introduced when they solve a meaningful business or security requirement rather than simply because a feature exists.

Engineering approach

Design. Implement. Validate.

This is an evolving technical case study. Security capabilities will be described as implemented only after configuration and testing provide evidence of their behaviour. Future design goals are not presented as completed controls.
